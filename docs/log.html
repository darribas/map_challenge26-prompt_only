Log

The exploration log, written along the way

Claude kept this log in LOG.md throughout the project, adding an entry only when the human asked for one. It’s the most compact record of what was tried, the numbers behind each decision and the dead ends. It’s included here as it stands in the repository (without its own title).

2026-11-04 — Contiguity graph (all four nations, queen)

Data (2024): 46,844 small areas — England 33,755 LSOAs, Scotland 7,392 Data Zones, Northern Ireland 3,780 Super Data Zones, Wales 1,917 LSOAs. EPSG:27700, 64 embedding bands (band{1..64}_mean), no missing values. Embedding vector lengths are 0.75–0.99 rather than 1, because they are averages of unit-length pixel vectors.

Queen contiguity (Graph.build_contiguity(lsoas, rook=False), ~18 s): 272,424 links (counted in both directions), but only 1 England–Scotland pair. Wales–England looked fine (99 links each way). strict=True was worse: zero England–Scotland links, and 326 fewer within Northern Ireland.

Diagnosis: polygons within 1 m of each other show ~23 England–Scotland pairs, all at distance 0, which overlap by up to ~70,000 m² (the two nations’ boundaries come from different sources). Contiguity requires polygons to touch without overlapping, so these links are dropped.

Fix: Graph.build_fuzzy_contiguity(lsoas), which links any polygons that intersect (~3 s). It contains every queen link plus the 21 missing England–Scotland pairs (23 in total), and nothing else changed.

Final graph g: 272,466 links, median 6 neighbours per area (max 53), 12 areas with no neighbours, 31 disconnected pieces (mainland GB 42,777; Northern Ireland 3,779; island groups, largest 89). The areas with no neighbours need handling before we compute dissimilarity.

2026-11-04 — Neighbour dissimilarity (cosine)

Measure: cosine distance between the 64-band embeddings of every pair of neighbours in g (fuzzy contiguity), via sklearn.metrics.pairwise.paired_cosine_distances. Chosen over Euclidean because the area vectors are averages of unit-length vectors: their length partly reflects how mixed the land cover is, and cosine ignores it. Each pair is computed in both directions (272,466 values). Results are stored as weights of g_dist = graph.Graph(Series(dist, index=g.adjacency.index)).

Parallelism: the areas are split into 16 groups by focal area (one per core), and each group’s pair distances are computed on a separate thread (joblib backend="threading"), then written back in the original order. Benchmark (scratch): serial ~1 s; separate processes (loky) were slower (1.6–2.2 s, because each worker has to be sent its share of the data); threads were ~4–8× faster. Bigger win: lsoas[band_cols].to_numpy() stores values column by column, so picking out whole rows is slow; np.ascontiguousarray(emb) cut the threaded step from ~0.66 s to ~0.16 s. The full notebook cell still takes ~0.75 s (not investigated). Checked: every pair computed once, matches the serial result, same structure as g.

Distribution: median 0.032, IQR 0.016–0.068, mean 0.054, max 0.54. Strongly right-skewed: most neighbours are similar, with a long tail of very different pairs.

2026-11-04 — Neighbourhood dissimilarity: mean_dist vs lag_dist

Two per-area scores, with equal weights for every neighbour: mean_dist = mean of the cosine distances to each neighbour (g_dist.aggregate("mean")); lag_dist = cosine distance to the average neighbour embedding (g.transform("r").lag(emb)). lag_dist <= mean_dist for every area. Spearman rank correlation 0.84. Medians 0.046 vs 0.017, max 0.456 vs 0.438. In the scatter plot, areas near the diagonal are outliers (unlike all their surroundings); areas well below it are transitions (their neighbours differ from each other). Also built most_dissimilar: each area’s single most dissimilar neighbour and the distance to it.

Top area on both measures: E01030745, a pair of reservoir basins just south of Heathrow, surrounded by housing/industry/airport (distances to those neighbours 0.39–0.54); its only similar neighbour (0.16) is the large reservoir to the west. Expect other water/coast cases near the top of the ranking.

2026-11-04 — Map template (style “C1”)

Iterated in the scratchpad: map of a focal area + its neighbours over satellite imagery, comparing 4 fades (dark, washed, greyscale, navy) × 5 line palettes, then an Imago-palette grid (colours taken from imago.ac.uk CSS: indigo #24226f/#181651, orange #ff8f42, teal #03cea3, lilac #a9afff, grey-lavender #8c91a8, amber #ffaf39). Chosen: C1 = Esri.WorldImagery; outside the focal area + neighbours washed to white (55%); focal outline orange, neighbours teal, both with a faint dark outline; UK locator inset with no box in Imago indigo with an orange dot.

Implementation (notebook, plot_focal(code)): fetch tiles once with cx.bounds2img and reproject to EPSG:27700 with cx.warp_tiles. The wash is applied to the image pixels with a mask from shapely.contains_xy against the merged outline of the focal area and its neighbours (this also allows effects like greyscale, which an overlay polygon can’t do). Attribution uses the provider’s own string.

UK outline for the inset: merging all 46k polygons took ~8 min. shapely.coverage_union_all on everything fails (the polygons overlap at national borders), so: merge each nation with coverage union (England 0.4 s, Scotland 3 s, Wales <0.1 s; Northern Ireland isn’t cleanly tiled → falls back to union_all on 200 m-simplified polygons), then join the four nations, drop holes and parts under 20 km², and simplify by 1.5 km. ~26 s total.

2026-11-04 — Ideas for more interesting cards + choosing k

Pair cards were one-note (built-up vs open land every time). Two ideas: (1) restrict the search to cities (needs boundaries: ONS area→local-authority lookup recommended, or built-up-area polygons); (2) cluster the embeddings, interpret the clusters visually, then search at cluster level (top pair per cluster combination, and “surprising neighbours”, i.e. cluster pairs adjacent less often than chance). Plan: do (2) first, then (1) as a filter on top.

k scan (k-means on vectors scaled to length 1, i.e. clustering by cosine similarity; all 46,844 areas; n_init=3; silhouette on a 10k sample, cosine; ~5 s per k):

k silhouette smallest largest
5 0.290 5,491 13,410

20 0.214 835 3,584

Silhouette falls steadily with no peak or elbow: the embeddings form continuous gradients, so k is a choice about interpretability, not a statistical answer. Very small clusters appear between k=40 and k=50, probably rare land types (water, airports, quarries). Decision: k=20 for labelling (190 cluster pairs, all clusters ≥835 areas), and also record each area’s k=50 cluster to keep the rare types. Re-run in the clusters notebook (isolates dropped, 46,832 areas): k=20 silhouette 0.212, smallest 842; k=40 → 282; k=50 → 195; k=75 → 72; k=100 → 67. So the sharp drop in smallest-cluster size sits between k=50 and k=75 there (the scratch run put it at 40→50, with 89 at k=50): its position is sensitive to the isolates and the random start. The fine level (k=50 vs k=75) is still open. Development moves to a second notebook, gdsl30dmc_clusters.ipynb; shared tooling moved to gdsl30dmc_tools.py.

Card options (gdsl30dmc_tools.py): - pair_distances() and neighbourhood_distances() compute every neighbouring-pair distance and per-area mean_dist/lag_dist once, cached to data/pair_distances_2024.parquet and data/neighbourhood_distances_2024.parquet (match notebook 1). - hist=True (pairs) or hist="lag_dist"/"mean_dist": slim histogram of the measure over the whole dataset in the caption strip, the card’s value marked with a line and “top x% of N”.
